\begin{abstract}
Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) classification from resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) remains difficult because functional-connectivity representations are high dimensional, multisite acquisition introduces substantial heterogeneity, and high average discrimination does not necessarily imply reliable confidence on individual predictions. A reliability-oriented connectome framework is therefore proposed using Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange I (ABIDE-I) data. Functional connectivity is represented through regularized covariance matrices and tangent-space projection, while static and windowed connectivity descriptions are evaluated under leakage-controlled pooled and leave-one-site-out cross-validation. Predictions obtained from multiple connectivity descriptions are combined, and a regularized linear classifier is used so that whole-brain decision patterns remain directly interpretable. \reviewhl{Probability calibration is assessed and selective prediction is evaluated so that the consequences of withholding uncertain cases are quantified.} The methodology is designed to test whether improved reliability \reviewhl{is obtained} through representation, cross-site validation, \reviewhl{calibration assessment}, and compact connectome structure rather than through classifier complexity alone. The wider quality-control cohort reaches an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.79 and an accuracy of \reviewhl{0.71} for the fixed connectivity combination, while cross-site performance remains nearly unchanged. The framework is intended as a \reviewhl{research-evaluation} bioinformatics pipeline rather than a replacement for clinical diagnosis. \reviewhl{[AUTHORS TO RUN: To retain ``Calibrated'' in the title, estimate calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, Brier score, and uncertainty intervals from genuinely held-out predictions; if recalibration is performed, fit it only within each outer-training partition and evaluate it on the corresponding held-out partition. Otherwise revise the title and calibration claims to ``calibration-assessed'' throughout.]}
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data availability}
This study analyses the Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange I (ABIDE-I), which is publicly
available through the ABIDE Preprocessed Initiative. The regional time series used here are the
C-PAC \texttt{filt\_noglobal} derivatives distributed with that release, together with the
accompanying phenotypic table. No new data were collected. Access is governed by the ABIDE
data-use terms, and no participant-level data are redistributed with this article. The
participant identifiers of the analysed cohorts, the fold assignments, and the derived result
tables are provided so that every reported value can be traced to its source.

\section*{Code availability}
The analysis code, the configuration files, and the stored result tables that generate every
table and figure in this article are available from the corresponding author on request and are
being prepared for deposit in a public repository with an archived release, subject to the
ABIDE data-use terms.

\section*{Ethics approval and consent}
This study is a secondary analysis of a de-identified, publicly available dataset. Ethical
approval and informed consent were obtained by the contributing sites of the ABIDE-I
consortium under their own institutional review boards, as described in the ABIDE-I data
descriptor. No additional ethical approval was required for the present analysis, and the
authors had no contact with participants.
